Sheet 05 — Case study

NYC public school SAT performance

New York City students sit the SAT each year, scored out of 800 in math, reading and writing. Working from a single table of 375 public high schools, I answered three questions that parents, researchers and policymakers tend to ask: who leads in math, who leads overall, and where results vary most.

ToolsPython, pandas
Dataschools.csv, 375 schools
SourceDataCamp project
SkillsFiltering, derived columns, groupby aggregation

What I found

10

schools score at least 640 in math (80% of the 800 maximum). Stuyvesant High School leads with 754.

2,144

is Stuyvesant’s combined score out of 2,400, the highest in the city. Bronx High School of Science and Staten Island Technical High School tie for second at 2,041.

Manhattan

has the widest spread in combined scores: a standard deviation of 230.29 across 89 schools, with an average of 1,340.13.

How I approached it

  • Filtered schools whose math average is at least 80% of 800, then sorted from highest to lowest.
  • Created a total_SAT column by summing the three section averages for each school, then ranked the top 10.
  • Grouped by borough with groupby and transform to get the school count, mean and standard deviation, rounded to two decimals, and kept the borough with the largest standard deviation.

The code and output

Taken from my DataCamp Workspace notebook for this project.

Load the data and answer all three questions
import pandas as pd

# Read in the data
schools = pd.read_csv("schools.csv")

# Preview the data
schools.head()

# Finding schools with the best math scores
best_math_schools = schools[schools["average_math"] >= 0.8 * 800][["school_name", "average_math"]].sort_values("average_math", ascending=False)

# Creating a column to calculate the total score for the schools
schools["total_SAT"] = schools[["average_math", "average_reading", "average_writing"]].sum(axis=1)

# Find the Top 10 schools based on the total score 
top_10_schools = schools[["school_name", "total_SAT"]].sort_values("total_SAT", ascending=False).head(10)

# Group the schools by the mean, count and standard deviation for each NYC borough
schools["num_schools"] = schools.groupby("borough")["school_name"].transform('count').round(2)
schools["average_SAT"] = schools.groupby("borough")["total_SAT"].transform('mean').round(2)
schools["std_SAT"] = schools.groupby("borough")["total_SAT"].transform('std').round(2)
largest_std_dev = schools[["borough", "num_schools", "average_SAT", "std_SAT"]].sort_values("std_SAT", ascending=False).head(1)

print(best_math_schools)
print(top_10_schools)
print(largest_std_dev.head())
