Sheet 05 — Case study

Grocery store product data

FoodYum, a US grocery chain, wants to keep stocking products across a range of prices as food costs rise. I worked through four tasks on its products table: counting records with a missing year, cleaning the table to match a data description without changing the original, finding price ranges by product type, and pulling high-selling meat and dairy lines.

ToolsSQL, PostgreSQL
Dataproducts table, 1,700 rows
SourceDataCamp practical exam
SkillsData cleaning, imputation, subqueries, medians

What I found

170

of the 1,700 products have no year_added value.

$3.46–$8.78

is the price range for produce, the lowest of any type. Meat is the highest at $11.48–$16.98.

698

meat and dairy products average more than 10 units sold a month.

How I approached it

  • Counted missing years with an IS NULL filter.
  • Cleaned every column in one SELECT: standardized text with INITCAP, UPPER and NULLIF, filled gaps with COALESCE, and imputed missing weights and prices with the median from PERCENTILE_CONT.
  • Used GROUP BY for the price range per product type and a filter for meat and dairy above 10 units sold.

Taken from my DataCamp Workspace notebook for this project.

Task 2: clean the product table
SELECT
	product_id,
	CASE
		WHEN INITCAP(product_type) IN ('Produce', 'Meat', 'Dairy', 'Bakery', 'Snacks') THEN INITCAP(product_type)
		ELSE 'Unknown'
	END AS product_type,
	COALESCE(NULLIF(brand, '-'), 'Unknown') AS brand,
	ROUND(COALESCE(
		REPLACE(weight, ' grams', '')::NUMERIC,
		(SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY REPLACE(weight, ' grams', '')::NUMERIC) FROM products)::NUMERIC
	), 2) AS weight,
	ROUND(COALESCE(
		price::NUMERIC,
		(SELECT PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY price::NUMERIC) FROM products)::NUMERIC
	), 2) AS price,
	COALESCE(average_units_sold, 0) AS average_units_sold,
	COALESCE(year_added, 2022) AS year_added,
	CASE
		WHEN UPPER(stock_location) IN ('A', 'B', 'C', 'D') THEN UPPER(stock_location)
		ELSE 'Unknown'
	END AS stock_location
FROM products;

Task 3: price range by product type
SELECT
	product_type,
	MIN(price) As min_price,
	MAX(price) AS max_price
FROM products
GROUP BY product_type
ORDER BY product_type
